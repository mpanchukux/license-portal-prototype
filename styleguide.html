Design system

Back to the portal

Every token and component the portal is built from, rendered from the same styles.css the pages load — nothing here is a copy or a screenshot. Change a rule and this page changes with the product. Each specimen is labelled with the class you write to get it.

Tokens

The variables on :root. Swatches and sizes are read from the live stylesheet at load, so they cannot drift from the values the product uses.

Colour

Two layers, and a component may read only the second. Primitives (--c-green-600, --c-red-600, --c-grey-600) are raw values named by hue and step; nothing reads them directly. Meanings (--status-ok, --status-alert, --status-off) are what a rule reads.

Colour is never the only carrier. Every coloured mark ships with a differently shaped glyph and, where there is room, the word: status is circle-check · circle-x · alert-triangle-filled, the Home banner is alert-circle · clock · alert-triangle. Remove the colour and every one of them still reads.

Two text greys, not three. A text colour clears 4.5:1 on its ground, and a glyph that identifies a control or its state clears 3:1 — which is the floor the third grey could not meet. Every text reader takes --mid; where a stack needs a third level it is carried by size. #9a9a9a exists under two roles only — --glyph-decorative (aria-hidden glyphs that carry no meaning alone) and --text-inactive (text inside a disabled control). A glyph that identifies a control or its state is not decorative.

--accent, --tick and --page-bg are literals and do not go through the primitive layer.

Type scale

Nine levels across two tiers plus the key, a 14px floor, and weight that grows with size (700 only on display and h1). The compact tier is what ≤600px substitutes for h1, h2 and body. display has no user in the product today.

Components

Nine components in three groups. Every entry has the same shape: what it is, its axes and the values on each, its states, its specimens, and its rules. The reasoning behind each decision is in COMPONENTS.md; the scales the components read are in SCALES.md.

Rules for every component

  • A component reads meanings, never the primitive layer. Four tokens are off limits; COMPONENTS.md and SCALES.md name them.
  • A form that cannot be justified as a variant is not built.

Icons

One closed set, and nothing else draws. Every icon is a <symbol> in assets/icons.svg, generated from @tabler/icons 3.48.0 by tools/build-icons.py. No inline <path>, no emoji, no text glyph, no second library — and tools/check-icons.py fails the pass if one appears.

How an icon is placed

The same four lines everywhere. The path is relative, because the site has to work from a subfolder.

<svg class="ic" aria-hidden="true">
  <use href="assets/icons.svg#ti-device-desktop"></use>
</svg>

In markup built by JS there is a helper that writes exactly that: icon('device-desktop', { size:20, cls:'gb-ic', label:'…' }).

Three sizes .ic / .ic-20 / .ic-24

16 is the base and needs no class. There is no fourth size.

Colour

An icon is the colour of the text it sits in. The symbol paints with currentColor and no rule anywhere may paint one — set color on the thing that contains it.

Labelling

An icon that carries meaning on its own gets role="img" and a name. An icon beside a text label is aria-hidden, because announcing it reads the same thing twice.

The whole set

Everything available, with the name to ask for it by. An icon the prototype needs that is not here is added to the sprite from Tabler under its official Tabler name and reported in the pass that adds it. If no Tabler icon fits, the pass stops and asks — nothing is drawn by hand.

Buttons

Every cell below is rendered by button() — nothing on this page is drawn by hand, so the matrix cannot disagree with the product. The axes, the states and the rules are under Components.

Ghost is the same button without the resting border. The two share one declaration for hover and press; the difference is whether the boundary is resting or summoned. The border is --mid, which clears 1.4.11’s 3:1 on every ground in the product — that threshold is what a replacement has to meet.

raised is the one button that answers the pointer by rising. The test: a surface may answer the pointer by rising only if it neither carries rows of data nor stands among them, and carries no more than one control — or if the surface is itself the thing being chosen. .blockmore-go fails it; the landing hero passes.

The matrix variants down, states across

The last column is the icon-only form, sized from the icon plus its own padding rather than from the label form narrowed. At md a ghost or text one is 54×40, a secondary one is 62×40, and menu is 38×40.

An icon-only button carries a grey glyph — --mid at rest, --ink on hover and press. A secondary with a label keeps ink, where the word carries the contrast.

Radius: 24px on everything with a label, a stadium on everything without one. The browser clamps 999px to half the shorter side, and clamps 24 to 20 at md — so only lg shows 24 as an actual corner.

Sizes three, and the third has one user by design

sm 26 — actions inside an ink banner. md 40 (--btnH) — everything standing in a control row. lg 48 — the page’s own primary beside its h1, one call site. Below 600px sm and md both resolve to 44, and that rule lives in the component, once. No call site says anything about size.

Tone default · destructive

An axis, not a variant: the variant decides the shape, the tone decides the colour, from --status-alert. The product needs a destructive primary (the confirm in the cancel dialog) and a destructive text item (Delete in a kebab).

States without colour

Hover and pressed are two different surfaces, not two opacities of one. On a filled button hover goes flat black and pressed adds an inset well at the top edge. On a quiet filled one (menu) hover and pressed are the next two steps of that fill’s own ramp. On a transparent one (secondary, text, ghost) hover takes --hover and pressed takes --sel.

Every icon button in a list row is the same one, so a row’s actions read the same wherever you are.

Focus is a ring and it is not hover: :focus-visible only — a mouse click does not raise it.

Disabled does not respond. Every hover and active rule is guarded.

Busy is the sixth state, and it is not disabled. Disabled means you cannot do this; busy means this is happening. It reserves its own width with the hidden label, keeps the accessible name, sets aria-busy, and takes aria-disabled rather than the disabled attribute, so focus is not lost mid-action. Restoring is the default. The 600ms refresh spin is this state at a shorter duration.

Not allowed, on purpose

The component refuses these rather than leaving them undocumented:

  • menu with a label.
  • menu at lg.
  • busy together with disabled — busy wins.

Error state .field.err > .fielderr

The error colour is --status-alert — the red that already means act now on Blocked and Past due, not a second red. It clears 4.5:1 as text.

Colour is not the only carrier: the border thickens and turns, the label turns with it, and the message is words. The message says what is wrong, not that something is — “Use MM / YY” can be acted on, “invalid” cannot. .help is hidden while an error shows, and the message carries no glyph.

The message is a caption (--t-caption, 13/1.35), and .field.err drops its bottom margin to pay for it. Use --t-caption for something that sits under something else, and for nothing else.

A primary action is never left disabled with nothing said. Either it is enabled and answers on submit — painting every failure at once and moving focus to the first — or it is disabled with the reason visible beside it. Fields answer on blur, and typing again withdraws the answer.

Status chips

A licence’s status is a coloured word (statusMark) — the word alone, in the colour of its state. The chips below read by weight and border: attention states take the ink fill, quiet states stay outlined.

The licence status mark

The word, in its own colour, with its own glyph. --status-ok for Active, --status-off for Canceled, --status-alert for Blocked. Three states, three outlines — circle-check, circle-x, alert-triangle-filled — so removing the colour leaves the column readable. All three clear AA as text (4.5:1); the ratios are in CONTRAST.md.

The line that must not be crossed: the moment two statuses share a word and differ only in hue, this cell fails.

Canceled, one L — the spelling every other surface uses, including the dialog that sets it.

Table

The licence table, rendered by the same builders the Licenses page calls (headHtml() / rowHtml()) — header, sortable column, a row with its actions, and the pager.

A table is a framed object: the head is a filled --surface-quiet band and the whole table sits inside its own hairline with a 10px radius. Home, Licenses, Invoices and the flat Instances list are all this one look. A row under the cursor takes --row-hover, and it is transient: nothing is selected, the pointer is simply there.

The specimen carries .listframe, because that is what the frame is scoped to.

The frame comes off below 600px — every row is already its own card there. A collapsed group has no outline at all. Grouped rows are inset 12px from the frame.

The frame is four bands, in this order

Toolbar · column header · rows · pagination footer. One block, one surface, one hairline round the outside. The first two stick — the toolbar (.stickybar) under the app bar, the column row (.stickyhead) directly beneath it — and rows pass under both for the whole length of the list. The footer is not sticky.

The footer shows only what is true. The count always shows; the page arrows and the items-per-page control appear only above one page, hidden rather than removed. An empty list has no footer at all, and the frame closes itself again — the last row takes its bottom corners back.

Below 600px the footer sheds what will not fit: the items-per-page control and the First / Last buttons go, the count takes the free space, prev and next stay.

Activity has no footer — it does not page, it is a log. Every other list is the framed pattern above. Users has no pager either: that list is four people by design.

Items per page 1–2 of 2

The specimen is showing the MULTI-PAGE footer. Its arrows and per-page control are in the markup by hand, because this page never runs syncPager; a real list of two rows shows the count alone.

Sortable header: th.sortable[aria-sort] · row actions: .cellact .lic-actions · footer: .listframe .pager

The sticky list block .listframe

One frame. Toolbar, column row and rows are one block: one thing to read, and on scroll one thing that stays. Nothing loose sits between the page title and the frame. Every list page uses it — Licenses, Instances, Invoices, Activity, Users — and so does the licence panel’s tab bar, in its own scroll.

What happens on scroll. The app bar is already fixed and the page title scrolls away like any content. The toolbar stops at the bottom edge of the app bar; the column row stops directly beneath it; rows pass under both.

The scroll container is #shellMain, not the document, so top:0 already means “under the app bar” and no header height is hard-coded. The column row’s own offset is the toolbar’s measured height (--barH), because the toolbar is a different height on every page.

A horizontal scroller is a sticky container too. overflow-x:auto computes overflow-y to auto as well, so the wrapper is made scrollable only when the table actually overflows it (.tablescroll.is-scrollable). Sideways scrolling and a stuck column row cannot both happen, and the width decides which.

The shadow --stickyShadow

A depth cue, not a border. It appears only once content has actually gone under the block and is gone at rest. One token, used by the page frames and by the licence panel’s tab bar:

Stuck block
a row passing underneath
another row

Drawn by whichever row is LAST in the stuck block — under the column row where there is one, under the toolbar where there is not. Two stacked shadows would read as two blocks.

“Stuck” is not “scrolled at all.” The test is a rect comparison against where the element actually comes to rest, and that resting line is not the container’s top edge: a scroll container’s padding insets its scrollport, so a sticky child parks that far down.

Below 600px

Only the column row sticks; the toolbar scrolls away with the title. Three stuck rows would take a third of a phone screen.

On every list today that means nothing sticks at all, and it is not a gap in the rule: Licenses, Instances, Invoices and Users turn their rows into cards at this width and hide thead with them, and Activity is a feed with no column row. The frame is also not a surface at this width: the rows carry a card each.

Licences toolbar

Search, a Type dropdown, a Status dropdown, and an independent Needs attention chip beside them. Needs attention is its own control rather than a fourth value of Status, because a licence can be Blocked and need attention.

The dropdown is Activity’s dropdown. Same trigger (.dropwrap.perctl + .btn--secondary.perbtn), same panel (.dropmenu), same rows (.dropcheck). The one difference is arity: Activity’s event types are multi-select and lead with a checkbox; these are single-select and end with a tick.

The two menus LIC_TYPE_OPTS / LIC_STATUS_OPTS

Rendered from the same two lists the page reads, so this specimen cannot fall behind what the filters offer. Counts are of the account, not of the current view — which is why they add up to the number in the page title.

The Status cell holds two axes. A STATE (Active · Blocked · Canceled, the three words the chip can print) and a CONDITION (Payment failed · Over instance limit · Updates ending, which replaces the date line). Wizard box — two presentations .fs-screen[data-present]

What differs is one thing: what the header and the stepper stand on. The steps, the content, the state and the controller are identical; a single attribute changes the two bands and nothing else.

C · White header band
Default. The frame is the viewport; the header and the stepper are a white band across the top, and the gradient starts below them. The stepper has no bottom hairline (2026-09-28) — the change of ground is the edge, and a grey line drawn on it was a third boundary in a header that has two.
D · Header on the gradient
The same frame with both bands transparent, so the gradient runs from the very top edge and the title and the stepper sit directly on it. The header keeps its hairline, because it is now the only thing separating the two bands.

What holds in both: no backdrop (the frame IS the viewport), so the close control and Escape are the only ways out; the two bands stay put while the body scrolls; focus is trapped inside and returns to whatever opened the wizard. Below 600px every .fs-screen is already a full-screen sheet, so C and D are identical there in every respect but the one being compared — which is why D’s rule is written outside the min-width:601px block.

Wizard stepper

One stepper: every step at once, each a numbered circle and a name, joined by a line. Only the circle of the current step is filled.

The list is computed, never written down. The steps are built per mode — Choose your plan · Capacity · Add-ons · Review, plus Payment & Billing when the account has no billing data — and steps drop out of it: a free plan has no Capacity, no Add-ons and no billing, TBMQ subscriptions have no Add-ons, and Manage add-ons has no plan step. The stepper renders steps(), so it cannot show a step the flow does not have.

Three states .nl-steps > .nl-step

Done is a ringed number in ink, current is a filled circle, upcoming is a ringed number in grey.

The fill is ink, not the accent. The accent is spent on the Popular plan card, where it means “we suggest this”; a filled step says you are here.

The connector carries progress. The line behind a step is solid ink and the line ahead of it is grey, so how far along you are is readable from the row’s own shape — no glyph, nothing that depends on colour. The line into the current step counts as travelled.

Done steps are clickable; the current one is where you are, and an upcoming one cannot be jumped to because the next step is computed from what this one holds. gotoStep only re-renders.

Below 900px the names go — circles joined by lines still read as a stepper. Below 600px only the current step is shown, named.

Activity

Every activity entry in the product renders through one component (activityEntry), from one copy map (ACTIVITY_TEXT). Home’s block, the Activity page and a licence’s own tab all call it; no page assembles a row.

A feed is an axis with entries hanging off it (activityList): the entry renders a row with a mark, the list renders the day separators between the runs and draws the rail. The specimens below call activityEntry directly, without the list, which is why they print a time and no date, and no rail.

The shape, in four parts.

  • The day is a separator ROW on the axis, not a heading beside the rows.
  • An entry is plain text with a mark on the axis, not a card.
  • A box appears only when there is more than the sentence.
  • The time trails the sentence after a middot.

The mark is keyed on the type’s PREFIX, with three named exceptions — payment failed, check failed, and updates expiring — so a new event type gets the right mark without anyone adding a line. The disc is filled --accent; the two things that can go wrong take --status-alert. The disc is a container and what it holds is a shape: the glyph differs per kind and the sentence says it in words, so no category is carried by hue alone. There is no per-category palette.

Anatomy

What it is about · what happened · what changed, from and to · who did it · the time. A reason or an amount sits inside the sentence, never only behind the expander — a failure that hides its cause behind a click is a failure you have to go and ask about.

The day is a separator, the time trails the sentence. The entry keeps only 07:12, after the sentence, and the date is a row of its own above the run of entries that share it. The entry is not self-sufficient because of that, which is why every surface goes through activityList and only this page does not.

A changed value is drawn as the change: the old value struck through and quiet, an arrow, the new one in a .pill — the plain pill, not a coloured one. The pill is the only enclosed thing in the row, so the eye lands on it; the struck value is there to be checked rather than read.

One chip per entry, always the same thing. In the account-wide feed the entity sits in a chip (.fi-chip) — the licence label, the instance label, the user’s email, the invoice number. Everything else is plain, values included. A chip says “this is a named thing”, not “this matters more”. Two entries have no chip at all: a password change and an invite link have no named subject but the account itself.

In a licence’s own tab the entity is dropped, not chipped again. In the map it is one template: the bracketed segment ([ on {entity}]) is what the licence scope removes. For an instance event the bracket holds the licence and the instance keeps the chip.

Voice. Past tense, no auxiliary: Plan changed from Startup to Business, never Plan was changed. One sentence, ending in a period. No “successfully”, no adjectives. A date inside a sentence uses the product’s one format, Sep 13, 2026.

Who did it is in the sentence, as {by}, and the template says where. A machine event has no actor at all, rather than saying “System”: the absence of a name is what says nobody did it.

No HTML in a string, ever. The copy says what happened; the component decides what is emphasised. That is what lets search match the sentence directly.

The expander is not a raw record. It shows named field · from → to rows, and only for the types that have something to show; a type with no detail gets no button. It must never repeat the sentence.

1 · Sentence and actor the default

Somebody did something. One sentence, the actor at the end, the time at the right. No panel, nothing to expand — most of the map is this.

2 · With a detail panel rec.detail

The sentence carries the headline change; the panel carries what the sentence left out, as named field · from → to rows. It must never repeat the sentence.

3 · No actor a machine event

Check-ins, expiries, automatic charges and the credit a downgrade returns. The sentence simply ends rather than saying “System”. An automatic charge is its own type, not a paid-invoice entry with the name left off.

4 · A grouped run instance.checks_grouped

Check-ins are derived, not stored — the licence reports hourly, so a nineteen-instance account would otherwise write hundreds of rows a day. An unbroken run of successful checks for the same instance becomes one entry carrying the count and both ends of the run.

It does not expand, and nothing in the feed does.

A failure never joins a run, even between two successes: it is the entry the reader is scanning for, and it names its cause in the sentence.

5 · A gap between two runs the structure is the message

A run covers consecutive checks. When an expected check never arrives, the run ends and a new one starts after the hole — so two groups with nothing between them are how the feed says a check is missing. Below: a run ending at 18:10 and the next beginning at 20:10, with no failure row between them. The 19:10 check never came.

The cadence is recorded, not guessed. The gap is measured against CHECKIN_INTERVAL_H — the interval the product states — and a delta below 1.5× it is treated as the same check arriving early or late. Only room for a whole missed slot breaks a run.

Info icon .infoic.tip.wide

The explanation a row’s name needs, one hover away — for a description worth reading once, to decide, on a row whose name and number get read every time. Built from infoIcon(about, text) in components.js; the text rides in data-tip, so there is no second copy of it in the DOM.

AI credits hover me

Rules. It is a <button>, so Tab reaches it and Enter opens it; aria-label names what is explained (“About AI credits”), never the explanation itself. Desktop opens on hover and :focus-visible. Below 600px there is no hover, so a tap toggles .show — one delegated handler in shared.js, which stands down entirely on a pointer device so a click never pins a bubble hover is about to show anyway. There the bubble also anchors to the heading rather than the glyph, which is what keeps a 300px bubble on a 375px screen.

When not to use it. If the description is what the reader is choosing between — an add-on they may or may not want — it stays as body text. Hiding it behind an icon would hide the thing being decided.

Alert icon .alertic.tip.wide

Two icons, two meanings. The info icon explains something neutral, and a reader who never opens it has lost nothing. This one says something is wrong.

The close control dismisses the lines it is showing, each under its own key, so they return independently at their own next threshold. The is absent whenever any line is blocking, not only when the first one is.

Dismissal is per licence AND per state. The key is <licence id>:<state>, so closing one notice can never silence a different licence, a different problem, or a later stage of the same one: B11:updates_30 and B11:updates_14 are different keys. The two “returns after N days” rules store the day dismissed, not a boolean.

Blocking states cannot be dismissed. Blocked, payment failed and no-payment-method are conditions where something has already stopped working or is about to. Time-based warnings come back on their own, so dismissing one costs nothing.

Home notifies; the licence records. The same conditions appear on the licence page in #subAlert and are never dismissible there.

A cancelled licence that is still active until its end date gets no banner. Nothing needs doing, and the state is already on the licence and in the list.

The same conditions mark the rows in the list, through the same function (attentionOf), so the banner and the filtered list cannot disagree about which rows belong in it. The two account-level conditions (no payment method, card expiring) have no licence to mark and appear in the banner only.

The Instances page instances.html

Columns: instance · licence · last check-in · version · status. Nothing about plans or money lives here — that is what the licence row is for.

Two views, and they do not have the same columns. A switcher on the page’s own toolbar picks between a flat list (instance · licence · last check-in · version · status) and grouped by licence (instance · status · version · last check-in). Grouped drops the License column because the heading above the rows is the licence.

The switcher sits after the spacer, beside Refresh: the controls on the left narrow the list and this one does not, so the toolbar reads as filters then presentation. It is not a chip pair — a chip group means “this is filtering” on both pages that carry one.

The first group is open, the rest are closed. The state holds only the groups a reader has actually toggled; everything else falls through to the index rule, which is what lets the first group be closed by hand and stay closed. The state is not stored — it is where a reader happens to be looking, not a setting. A search forces every group open, because rows are filtered in the DOM; headings whose every row was filtered out hide with them. Grouping and paging cannot both be on, and grouping wins.

A group is one outline, not a run of rows. Each group is a box with an 8px radius and a stroke that starts at the heading and closes under the last instance in it; groups are separated by space, not by a rule. Nothing inside is indented and nothing gains padding. The gap between groups is a spacer row. On the phone the group closes and becomes a heading — a labelled band over a stack of cards, no cards inside cards.

The id carries a ghost copy button (.ghostbtn.inst-copy): the id is a 36-character uuid that the cell ellipsizes, so the clipboard is the only way to get all of it.

It filters by the one thing an instance has: search plus a .inst-statusseg chip pair (Healthy / Stale), the same mutually exclusive group as the Licenses type filter. Version is not a filter — “behind latest” is a fact about a release, not a state of the deployment.

The empty state offers nothing. An instance is not something you create in the portal, so the only line is how one appears: a deployment somewhere activates itself with a key. No action.

On the phone the row is a card (.inst-row). Reading order is the deployment, the licence it draws on, then the three facts that answer “is it healthy” on one line.

Deactivate and Delete live on the instance row and nowhere else. The blocked banner routes here. Discovery is handled by copy, in two places: the blocked banner and a quiet persistent line above this list (.inst-hint).

The confirmation is destructive treatment, because this turns off live infrastructure. It names the instance, shows its full ID and last check-in, and says what happens and when: the server keeps running until its next check-in, within the hour, and then stops. The seat is freed immediately.

Where a message goes — two places, one rule each

A message has one place, decided by its kind. Nothing invents a third.

1 · Result of an action snackbar. Something the person just did, which is finished and will not become truer by staying on screen: plan changed, capacity purchased, add-on removed, coupon applied, label saved, payment method updated, subscription cancelled, updates renewed, invoice downloaded. It lives outside the licence panel — bottom of the viewport on desktop, top below 600px — and leaves on its own.

2 · Persistent state the slot below the panel header (#subAlert). Facts that stay true until something changes: payment failed, over the instance limit, cancelled and active until a date, software updates expiring. One at a time. If two are true, show the more serious one and name the other in the same banner. Nothing transient goes here.

The one banner that sits above the header is #licNewBanner — License created — so a newly created licence with a problem can show it and the state banner at once. It appears once per licence, ever: the seen mark is written when it is shown, not when it is dismissed.

Exception. License key copied and Instance ID copied answer in the button’s own tooltip, not the snackbar: a copy is a micro-action on a small control, and an answer at the edge of the screen makes the reader look away from the thing they just pressed.

Bottom sheet .dprofmenu · #headKebabPop · .permenu

Below 600px a menu with more than two items stops being an anchored dropdown and becomes a sheet at the bottom edge. Three of them exist: the profile menu, the licence the row overflow menu, and Activity’s period filter. One declaration covers all three — they had drifted apart and were each missing the same things.

The three requirements. A grab handle (36×4, centred) so the thing reads as a sheet rather than a panel that appeared; 22px under the last item, because a list ending flush against the screen edge gives no way to tell whether it continues; and scrolling at max-height:72vh, or a sheet taller than the viewport silently loses its tail. The handle is a ::before, so a sheet needs no extra markup to get one.

Snackbar .snack

A transient message that reports something happened, then leaves on its own (4.2s) — with a dismiss for the impatient. Two anchors, one per breakpoint: on the desktop it sits at the bottom of the viewport, in the margin below the content; below 600px it anchors to the TOP, 12px under the app bar, where there is one fixed thing to clear and nothing pinned below it. role="status", not alert — an alert interrupts.

Confirmations leave; problems stay. The snackbar carries completed actions. It never carries a field error: those stay inline beside the input, because the person reads them while fixing the thing they describe.
